# 30 Evidence-Backed Habits (Crossref-Verified)

> Verified 2026-08-13. All 18 academic citations in this list were checked against the Crossref API — titles, authors, journals, and years all confirmed. One correction: the original PREDIMED paper (NEJM 2013) was retracted in 2018 over randomization-allocation concerns, so item 11 cites the 2018 republication instead. Each entry carries a DOI for traceability.

## 1. Body

### 01 Move a little every day — 30 minutes isn't a magic number
- **Advice**: If you're sedentary, start with ~15 minutes/day of moderate activity and build up.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Wen CP et al. *Minimum amount of physical activity for reduced mortality and extended life expectancy.* The Lancet, 2011. DOI: 10.1016/s0140-6736(11)60749-6

### 02 Do strength training once or twice a week
- **Advice**: ~2 sessions of muscle-strengthening activity per week, not cardio only.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Momma H et al. *Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases.* Br J Sports Med, 2022. DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061

### 03 Don't worship "10,000 steps" — but walk more
- **Advice**: ~7,000 steps/day is a practical target for sedentary people.
- **Evidence**: A/B
- **Source**: Ding D et al. *Daily steps and health outcomes in adults: a systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis.* Lancet Public Health, 2025. DOI: 10.1016/s2468-2667(25)00164-1

### 04 Get up and move briefly during long sitting
- **Advice**: Break up prolonged sitting with a few minutes of activity every 30–60 minutes.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Dunstan DW et al. *Breaking up prolonged sitting reduces postprandial glucose and insulin responses.* Diabetes Care, 2012. DOI: 10.2337/dc11-1931

## 2. Sleep

### 05 Most adults should aim for 7–9 hours
- **Advice**: Treat 7–9 hours as a reasonable range; adjust based on daytime alertness.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Watson NF et al. *Recommended Amount of Sleep for a Healthy Adult.* SLEEP, 2015 (published simultaneously in J Clin Sleep Med). DOI: 10.5665/sleep.4716

### 06 Keep a consistent wake and sleep time
- **Advice**: Don't let your schedule drift dramatically on weekends.
- **Evidence**: B (sleep-hygiene consensus principle, no single DOI)

### 07 Cut caffeine in the afternoon and evening
- **Advice**: Caffeine-sensitive people can try stopping 6–9 hours before bed.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Drake C et al. *Caffeine Effects on Sleep Taken 0, 3, or 6 Hours before Going to Bed.* J Clin Sleep Med, 2013. DOI: 10.5664/jcsm.3170
- **Note**: Individual dose, genetics, and tolerance vary widely — not an absolute ban.

### 08 Don't doomscroll in bed
- **Advice**: Move your phone from "within reach" to "must get up to grab" before sleep.
- **Evidence**: B (the point is reducing information stimulation, not "blue light destroys everything")

## 3. Diet

### 09 Eat enough fruit and vegetables daily
- **Advice**: ~5 servings of fruit + vegetables per day, varied.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Wang DD et al. *Fruit and Vegetable Intake and Mortality.* Circulation, 2021. DOI: 10.1161/circulationaha.120.048996

### 10 "Eat the rainbow" is really about diversity
- **Advice**: Long-term variety of vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, and nuts — not literally matching colors.
- **Evidence**: A/B

### 11 Move toward a Mediterranean-style diet, not supplements
- **Advice**: More vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, nuts, olive oil; fewer ultra-processed foods.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Estruch R et al. *Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease with a Mediterranean Diet Supplemented with Extra-Virgin Olive Oil or Nuts.* NEJM, 2018 (republication). DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1800389
- **⚠ Note**: The original 2013 version (DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1200303) was retracted over randomization-allocation issues — cite the 2018 republication.

### 12 Don't start drinking "for your health"
- **Advice**: If you don't drink, there is no health reason to start.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: GBD 2016 Alcohol Collaborators. *Alcohol use and burden for 195 countries and territories, 1990–2016.* The Lancet, 2018. DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31310-2

## 4. Psychology

### 13 Practice mindfulness or meditation
- **Advice**: 5–10 minutes/day is enough; no need for 20.
- **Evidence**: A/B
- **Source**: Goyal M et al. *Meditation Programs for Psychological Stress and Well-being.* JAMA Intern Med, 2014. DOI: 10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.13018

### 14 When stressed, write down what actually bothers you
- **Advice**: Write your emotions, the event, and how you make sense of it — not just a stream of events.
- **Evidence**: B
- **Source**: Gortner EM et al. *Benefits of expressive writing in lowering rumination and depressive symptoms.* Behavior Therapy, 2006. DOI: 10.1016/j.beth.2006.01.004

### 15 Occasionally write down things you're grateful for
- **Advice**: Try a week or two of daily gratitude notes, then see if it helps.
- **Evidence**: A/B
- **Source**: Jackowska M et al. *The impact of a brief gratitude intervention on subjective well-being, biology and sleep.* J Health Psychol, 2016. DOI: 10.1177/1359105315572455

### 16 Walk outdoors when stressed
- **Advice**: Put some of your walks in nature — parks, woods, riverside.
- **Evidence**: B (no universal "daily minutes" dose)
- **Source**: Jimenez MP et al. *Associations between Nature Exposure and Health.* Int J Environ Res Public Health, 2021. DOI: 10.3390/ijerph18094790

## 5. Relationships

### 17 Actively maintain a few relationships that matter
- **Advice**: Stable contact with close friends, family, or a partner beats forcing yourself into lots of socializing.
- **Evidence**: A/B
- **Source**: Holt-Lunstad J et al. *Social Relationships and Mortality Risk: A Meta-analytic Review.* PLoS Med, 2010. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316

### 18 Don't chase "only positive people"
- **Advice**: Seek supportive, reliable, reciprocal relationships — not a "positive/negative energy" binary.
- **Evidence**: B

### 19 Alone time is fine — don't mandate "2 hours a week"
- **Advice**: Arrange quiet alone time as you need it, not around a nonexistent scientific standard.
- **Evidence**: C

## 6. Learning

### 20 Spread learning across days
- **Advice**: Use spaced practice instead of cramming.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Cepeda NJ et al. *Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks.* Psychological Bulletin, 2006. DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354

### 21 Actively recall after learning
- **Advice**: Close the book and restate, solve problems, or explain in your own words.
- **Evidence**: A (retrieval practice)

### 22 Don't mandate "one book a week"
- **Advice**: 20–30 minutes of daily reading aimed at understanding beats a 52-books/year quota.
- **Evidence**: C

### 23 Keep learning new, challenging things long-term
- **Advice**: Instrument, language, programming, photography, sport skills — the point is continuity, not "exactly one per year."
- **Evidence**: B/C

## 7. Environment & Behavior Design

### 24 Add friction to phone use
- **Advice**: Keep it far at night, kill non-essential notifications, move addictive apps off the home screen.
- **Evidence**: B/C (behavior design — not the "Stanford Attention Research Institute," which does not exist)

### 25 Write goals + concrete actions + accountability
- **Advice**: What will I do → when → what's the next step → who will know my progress.
- **Evidence**: B ("sticking it on the fridge" is not itself a validated technique)

## 8. Basic Health

### 26 Brush your teeth properly every day
- **Advice**: At least morning and night, plus baseline oral hygiene.
- **Evidence**: B
- **Source**: Park SY et al. *Improved oral hygiene care attenuates the cardiovascular risk of oral health disease.* Eur Heart J, 2019. DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehy836

### 27 Wash your hands at the right moments
- **Advice**: After the toilet, before cooking, before eating, and after touching obvious infection sources.
- **Evidence**: A
- **Source**: Luby SP et al. *Effect of handwashing on child health: a randomised controlled trial.* The Lancet, 2005. DOI: 10.1016/s0140-6736(05)66912-7

### 28 Don't smoke — quitting still pays off
- **Advice**: There is no "smoked too long, quitting is pointless now."
- **Evidence**: A

### 29 Avoid sunburn, not mechanically "15 minutes of sun daily"
- **Advice**: Use clothing, shade, and sunscreen during prolonged strong exposure.
- **Evidence**: A/B
- **Source**: Green AC et al. *Reduced melanoma after regular sunscreen use.* J Clin Oncol, 2011. DOI: 10.1200/JCO.2010.28.7078

### 30 Get age- and risk-appropriate screening
- **Advice**: Blood pressure, lipids, diabetes, and cancer screening should follow age, sex, family history, and personal risk — not "more tests = healthier."
- **Evidence**: A (risk–benefit assessment in preventive medicine)

# The 10 Most Worth Keeping

1. Exercise regularly, combining aerobic and strength training
2. Walk more daily, reduce prolonged sitting
3. Get about 7–9 hours of sleep
4. Keep a relatively regular schedule
5. Eat more vegetables, fruit, and minimally processed whole foods
6. Don't smoke; less alcohol is better
7. Maintain a few relationships that truly matter
8. Keep learning — use spacing + active recall instead of rote rereading
9. When stressed: exercise, get outdoors, meditate, or write expressively
10. Brush, protect from sun, wash hands, and get age-appropriate screening

Common thread: hard evidence, large long-term payoff, and no reliance on marketing numbers like "10 seconds, 15 minutes, 20%, a book a week, one a year."
